F1 corner speed analysis, made comparable

Compare how Formula 1 drivers and teams carry speed through each corner. Choose a Grand Prix session to explore turn-by-turn rankings, head-to-head laps, speed traces, sector times, and race pace—all in one free, browser-based analysis tool.

How this F1 telemetry analysis works

The analyzer requests session, lap, driver, and car telemetry from the OpenF1 API when you choose an analysis. For each driver, it selects the fastest eligible lap, converts speed samples into distance along the lap, and resamples the lap into 1,000 evenly spaced points so speeds can be compared at the same track position.

Corner apexes come from the circuit data when available; if that geometry cannot be loaded, the tool estimates corners from speed minima. Each corner is evaluated in a small window around its apex (about ±1.2% of lap distance) using average speed. Team corner scores average the analyzed drivers for that team. These are telemetry-based comparisons, not official classifications or claims about overall race performance.

Frequently asked questions

Which F1 sessions can I analyze?

Select an available Practice, Qualifying, Sprint, Sprint Shootout or Sprint Qualifying, or Race session. Availability depends on the season and on the sessions published by OpenF1.

Is the corner-speed comparison based on official F1 timing?

The app uses telemetry and session information served by OpenF1, an independent data API. The comparisons are calculated by this tool and are not official Formula 1 results.

Does the tool save my session data?

No. Session data is requested live and analyzed in your browser; the app does not store the fetched F1 data. You can export available analysis tables as CSV after an analysis has loaded.

Why might an analysis be slow or incomplete?

The tool makes live API requests and paces them to respect service limits. Older or incomplete sessions may have missing telemetry, and some results can take longer to load. Try analyzing fewer drivers if requests are slow.

13How this works

Best lap. For every driver we take the single fastest non-pit-out lap of the session you selected — nothing else from the weekend is used.

Same point on track. Speed samples arrive over time, but drivers reach each point at different times. We integrate speed into distance travelled and resample every lap to 1,000 evenly spaced points, so point 500 is the same piece of asphalt for everyone.

Corners. Apex positions come from official circuit data fetched live. Each corner is a small window (±1.2% of lap distance) around its apex.

Ranking. Within each window we average each driver's speed. Highest average wins the corner. A team's score is the mean of its drivers. "Corners won" counts windows topped.

Tyres & sectors. Compounds come from the session's stint list matched to the lap number of each best lap; sectors and traps are the official splits of that same lap.

Data: OpenF1 API · Unofficial tool, not affiliated with Formula 1 or OpenF1
